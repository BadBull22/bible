## 1. Desktop 2.6.1 — what changed

The reported problem: "the headings are so light they don't appear unless you hover". The
chapter heading's buttons (Pictures, Listen, Side by side, Study sheet, Copy chapter) used
`.text-btn`, whose colour was near-white because it was made for the dark navy top bar; on
the cream page they were invisible until hover. The same buttons were faint in panels, the
book reader and Settings.

- **`.text-btn`** now uses `var(--text-muted)` (follows the theme and contrast); only
  `.top-bar .text-btn` keeps the light colours. Disabled: `--text-faint` at 0.6 opacity.
- **`--text-faint`** was 2.9–3.1:1 on the light surfaces (below the 4.5:1 minimum for small
  text): now `#756e5f` (4.5:1) in light, `#9a958b` (5.7:1) in dark.
- **Settings → Appearance** (new): Theme (Match Windows / Light / Dark), Text colour
  (Standard / Strongest / Warm sepia / Blue-black), Contrast (Standard / High), and "Back
  to the standard look". Stored with the reading prefs in localStorage (`reading:prefs`:
  `theme`, `textColor`, `contrast`); `src/readingPrefs.ts` sets `data-theme` (always
  resolved to light/dark), `data-text` and `data-contrast` on `<html>`. Applied in
  `main.tsx` before the first render (no light flash). With "Match Windows", a Windows
  switch is followed live (`watchSystemTheme`).
- **The dark theme is no longer a `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block.** All three
  were rewritten as rules under `:where(:root[data-theme="dark"])` (`:root:where(...)` for
  the token block), which keeps each rule's specificity and place in the cascade, so dark
  looks exactly as before. **New dark-mode CSS must use the same form**, not a media query.
- The window title bar follows the theme: `getCurrentWindow().setTheme(...)`, permission
  `core:window:allow-set-theme` added to `src-tauri/capabilities/default.json`.
- Help → "Appearance, reading comfort and focus mode" describes it; searching "pale",
  "contrast" or "theme" finds it.

Checked: tsc, vite build, `cargo test` (53 passed), the owner in the dev app ("showing the
correct colours and it's visible").

## 4. Android ideas (owner reading the comparison; nothing started)

Compared with YouVersion, the ESV app and BibleProject. Suggested, in order:

1. Appearance settings matching the desktop (theme, text colour, contrast, sepia, font).
2. Daily reminder notification for the reading plan.
3. Reading streak and plan progress.
4. Verse images to share (verse over a background).
5. Read aloud: choose the phone's voice, speed, sleep timer.
6. Tap a word for Strong's (data exists in the desktop app).
7. One small public-domain commentary (e.g. Matthew Henry's Concise).
8. Two translations side by side for a whole chapter.
9. Optional downloadable public-domain translations (eBible.org), keeping the APK small.
10. Tags/folders for notes and bookmarks (the backup already carries desktop tags).
11. Home-screen widget (verse of the day).
12. Phone ↔ PC sync over home Wi-Fi without a server.
13. Memory-verse flashcards.

Deliberately not: accounts/community (needs a server), copyrighted translations or audio,
BibleProject videos (copyrighted, large; a link to their free online video per book is
possible).
