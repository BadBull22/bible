Privacy policy

Bible Concordance for Windows and Android · last updated 31 October 2026

In short: Bible Concordance has no account, no adverts, no tracking and no analytics. The developer has no server that your notes or details are sent to, and does not collect, receive, sell or share any personal information about you. Everything you write stays on your own device.

Who this covers

This policy covers the Bible Concordance app for Windows and the Bible Concordance app for Android, and this website (bible4me.org). The apps are made by Erich P. Tonsing (“the developer”) and are free. See the licence and terms.

What stays on your device

These are kept only on your own computer or phone, in the app's own storage, and are never sent to the developer or to anyone else by the app:

  • your bookmarks, highlights, notes, tags and reading-plan progress, and your reading streak;
  • on Windows, your sermons, slides, notices, songs and settings;
  • the Bibles, commentaries, books and other files you choose to add or download;
  • the backups you choose to save, and any key you type in for an optional online service (see below).

Uninstalling the Android app deletes its stored data. On Windows the data lives in the app's data folder, which you can back up or delete yourself. Because the developer never holds a copy, he cannot recover your notes if you lose them: save a backup now and then.

What we collect

Nothing. There is no sign-up, no profile, no advertising identifier, no analytics or crash-reporting service, and no location, contacts, camera, microphone or phone-number access.

When the apps do use the internet

Everything built in works offline. The apps go online only for the things below, and only when you use them or leave them switched on. When your device contacts one of these services, that service can see your internet address and the ordinary details of the request, as any website would, under its own privacy policy. The developer does not receive that information.

  • Looking for a new version. The Android app asks bible4me.org (hosted by GitHub Pages) once a day for a small file that names the newest version; you can switch this off or check by hand under More → Updates. The Windows app can check GitHub for a new version. Nothing about you or your device is sent beyond the request itself. Installing is always your choice.
  • Optional downloads you choose: more Bibles and library books (from eBible.org, CrossWire and the other free sources named in the app), the nature photos, the “Advanced search” and “Original words” packs (from GitHub), and Bible pictures (from Wikimedia Commons). What is downloaded is the file you picked.
  • Windows only: live NIV, NKJV and ESV (optional). If you type in your own key from the publisher's service (api.bible or api.esv.org), the app sends the passage you ask for, with your key, to that service so it can show you the text. The key is stored on your computer. The publisher's own terms and privacy policy apply to that service.
  • Windows only: looking up song words (optional). When you ask for a song's words, its title and artist are sent to the lookup service the app uses. Nothing is sent otherwise.
  • Links. Links to YouTube Music and similar sites open in your browser or app, and are governed by that site's policy.

Keeping your phone and computer in step

If you pair the Android app with the Windows app, they talk directly to each other over your own home Wi-Fi, using a code you type in. Your bookmarks, highlights, notes and reading plans do not go through the internet or through any cloud, and the developer cannot see them.

Android permissions, and why

  • Internet — for the optional downloads and the update check above.
  • Notifications and alarms — for the daily reading reminder you set. Start at boot and ignore battery optimisation — so the reminder still arrives after a restart and on time.
  • Install apps — so that, when you tap Install on an update, the app can hand the downloaded file to Android, which asks you to confirm.
  • Keep awake — so that the reminder and a download can finish when the screen is off.
  • Files you add or save go through Android's own file picker; the app can open only the files you pick.

Children

The apps are for anyone and are not aimed at collecting anything from children. Since nothing is collected from anyone, there is nothing to collect from a child either.

This website

The website is a set of plain pages hosted by GitHub Pages. It has no sign-up, no adverts and no analytics of its own. GitHub, as the host, may keep ordinary server logs under its own privacy statement. Downloads are served by GitHub Releases.

Your rights

Because the developer holds no personal information about you, there is nothing for him to show, correct, export or delete. You are in control of your data on your own device. If you believe otherwise, or you have a privacy question, please write on GitHub and you will get a straight answer.

Changes to this policy

If the apps ever start to work differently in a way that affects your privacy, this page will be updated first and the date at the top changed.
